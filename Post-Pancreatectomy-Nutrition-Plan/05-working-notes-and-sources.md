# Working Notes and Sources

## Working Principles

- Recovery comes first.
- Adequate calories and protein are more important than aggressive carb restriction right now.
- Avoid making the diet so low-carb that he under-eats.
- Target roughly 80 to 120g protein per day if appetite and tolerance allow.
- Use moderate carbs, not zero carbs.
- Avoid obvious sugar bombs.
- Prefer small frequent meals.
- Watch fat tolerance.
- Glucose monitoring matters, but do not overcorrect the diet prematurely.
- Splenectomy mainly affects infection risk, vaccines, and fever protocol rather than the grocery list.

## Why This Plan Looks This Way

Johns Hopkins pancreatic cancer nutrition guidance emphasizes maintaining weight, staying hydrated, eating small frequent meals, and including high-protein foods with meals and snacks.

MSK's distal pancreatectomy guide notes that after this surgery, some people can have diarrhea if the pancreas does not make enough digestive enzymes, and some can have high blood sugar if the pancreas does not make enough insulin.

MSK and PanCAN both describe pancreatic enzyme replacement therapy as a tool when digestion or fat absorption is impaired.

CDC guidance notes that people without a spleen are at increased risk for certain infections, especially encapsulated bacteria, so vaccine status and fever protocol should be clarified with the care team.
